# Persistence for Ten-Year-Olds

A deliberately flat pedagogical working repository for teaching the structure of **Persistence / the Constraint Grammar of Completed Intelligibility** to children.

The aim is not to simplify away the system. It is to give children a route into the same 6 × 3 × 3 structure through ordinary language, comparison, sorting, discussion, and recognition.

## Files

### `goldmine.md`

The generation instrument.

It gives an independent GPT the primitive grammar and asks for ten ordinary companion utterances for each of the 54 coordinates.

Its purpose is to expose each cell's natural utterance field: convergence, variation, recurring attractors, and possible instability.

Generated utterances are evidence and pedagogical material, **not automatically canonical answers**.

### `pixels_of_clarity.md`

The cleaned pedagogical corpus.

It merges three independent Goldmine runs:

- 1,620 raw utterances;
- 1,307 retained after conservative duplicate / near-duplicate consolidation;
- 313 collapsed as duplicates or close paraphrases.

Original retained wording is preserved. Structural disagreements are flagged rather than silently corrected.

This is the main source material from which the ten-year-old teaching system can now be built.

## Working pedagogical direction

The emerging route is:

**experience → comparison → discrimination → recognition → naming → map → transfer**

Children should first encounter structures in ordinary sentences, learn to compare and sort them, and only then acquire the formal names and the complete 54-position map.

The intended destination is a child who can use three structural questions:

1. **What kind of change happened?**
2. **What made it count as complete?**
3. **How does the achieved result remain available?**

Those questions lead into:

- 6 Transformation Patterns;
- 3 Completion Topologies;
- 3 Persistence Modes;
- 54 coordinates.

The search procedure may be taught sequentially for navigation, while the underlying cell remains a simultaneous conjunction of all three dimensions.

## Curriculum Commitment: What This Project Must Actually Teach

The game, cards, classroom discussion, parent activities, boxes, and computer interface are **pedagogy**.

The invariant object being taught is **Persistence / the Constraint Grammar of Completed Intelligibility**.

A successful pedagogical version must leave the learner with a usable grasp of the following structure.

### 1. Completed intelligibility

Persistence concerns transitions in which:

**something is unresolved in a determinate way → something answers that unresolvedness → the answer becomes sufficient → a determinate result remains available.**

“Complete” is indexed to the operative demand. It does not mean absolutely finished forever.

The learner should understand that the system is not primarily sorting topics, objects, emotions, verbs, or sentence meanings. It is sorting **forms of completed intelligibility**.

### 2. Transformation Pattern

**Transformation Pattern specifies what kind of unresolvedness is altered between the opening and completed situation.**

There are six:

**Differentiation**  
A difference that was unavailable, flattened, conflated, or non-operative becomes determinate enough to matter.

**Opening**  
Availability, reach, continuation, access, or possibility becomes greater than it was under the opening constraint.

**Relating**  
A bearing, dependency, correspondence, ordering, reciprocity, or mutual determination becomes constitutive of the situation.

**Repair**  
An organization that fails relative to an operative criterion is changed so that the misfit or fault no longer governs the result.

**Integration**  
Separate parts acquire determinate roles through organization into a whole and thereby gain structure, mutual support, or joint capacity.

**Settlement**  
A live demand for further uptake, choice, explanation, completion, adjudication, or continuation is discharged.

The learner should become able to recognize **which kind of unresolvedness has been transformed**.

### 3. Completion Topology

**Completion Topology specifies the structural shape by which the transformation counts as complete.**

There are three:

**Focal**  
The transformation becomes complete because completion gathers around one privileged locus. That locus is constitutive of why the transformation counts as complete.

**Distributed**  
The transformation becomes complete only across a constitutive plurality, span, sequence, range, reciprocal field, set of cases, or system. No single locus is sufficient by itself.

**Release**  
The transformation becomes complete because something that previously governed the situation loses its determining force.

**Release means de-governance, not merely removal, absence, negation, separation, or movement away.**

The learner should become able to recognize **what structural condition makes the transformation sufficient**.

### 4. Persistence Mode

**Persistence Mode specifies how the achieved completion remains available afterward.**

There are three:

**Standing**  
The achieved determination itself remains available as the completed result.

**Bearing**  
The achieved result remains what it is only through a constitutive relation, dependency, contrast, condition, source, proportion, frame, support, or mutual determination.

**Efficacy**  
The achieved result remains as an accomplished capacity, operative effect, changed continuation, rule, function, or consequential difference in what can happen.

The learner should become able to recognize **the mode in which the completion remains available**.

### 5. The three dimensions are different questions

The learner should grasp that these are three different kinds of structural question:

1. **Transformation Pattern** — What kind of unresolvedness changed?
2. **Completion Topology** — By what structural condition did it count as complete?
3. **Persistence Mode** — How does the achieved completion remain available?

The three-question procedure may be taught as a practical way to navigate the system.

### 6. Dimensional Conjunction

A coordinate such as:

**Integration × Distributed × Bearing**

does **not** mean:

Integration property + Distributed property + Bearing property.

The three dimensions are simultaneous constraints upon **one completion-event**.

The coordinate is a constraint that generates the form, not a description of three detachable properties possessed by the form.

The child may use the three questions sequentially to find a box, but should not be left thinking the underlying phenomenon is assembled in three sequential semantic stages.

A useful child-level formulation is:

> **The questions help us find the box. They are not instructions for building what happened.**

### 7. Non-Cartesian and non-operator discipline

The learner should eventually avoid the following false shortcuts:

- Focal does not merely mean “one thing.”
- Distributed does not merely mean “lots of things.”
- Release does not merely mean “something went away.”
- Standing does not merely mean “it stayed.”
- Bearing does not merely mean “there is a relationship.”
- Efficacy does not merely mean “something useful happened.”

No dimension simply operates on another.

Each term gets its exact realization within the unified completion represented by the cell.

### 8. The 54 positions

The formal space is:

**6 Transformation Patterns × 3 Completion Topologies × 3 Persistence Modes = 54 coordinates.**

Every position is one conjunction written:

**Transformation Pattern × Completion Topology × Persistence Mode**

The learner should understand why there are 54 positions and should develop a clear mental map of the system.

The objective is not rote memorization of cell numbers. It is recognition of what differentiates one structural position from its neighbours.

### 9. Relational discrimination between neighbouring cells

A cell is partly clarified by what neighbouring cells are not.

Changing one dimension while holding the other two fixed should change the identity of the completion, not merely its wording.

The learner should therefore practice distinctions such as:

- Repair × Focal × Standing
- Repair × Focal × Bearing
- Repair × Focal × Efficacy

and explain why they are genuinely different completed forms.

This neighbour discrimination is a central part of structureacy.

### 10. No completion, no coordinate

The grammar must not manufacture completion where none occurred.

**No completion, no coordinate.**

The learner should be allowed to say:

> “There is not enough here yet.”

Classification is not the same as forcing every sentence or fragment into a box.

### 11. Governing completion

A sentence or event may contain smaller local completions while having one governing completion.

At more advanced stages the learner should ask:

**What completion governs the intelligibility of the whole thing?**

The early curriculum may keep this distinction light, but it belongs to the system.

### 12. Intelligibility is not truth

A completed intelligibility may later prove false, mistaken, bad, or incorrect.

The grammar describes **how something becomes determinate enough to be judged**, not whether its content is true.

A coordinate therefore does not certify correctness.

### 13. Recognition in ordinary language and life

The learner should ultimately recognize these structures in ordinary sentences, conversations, decisions, repairs, explanations, games, relationships, arrangements, problems, actions, and stories.

The terminal competence is not:

> “I can recite 54 boxes.”

It is:

> **“I can notice a completion, identify its structural form, locate it in the grammar, distinguish it from its neighbours, and explain what I noticed.”**

### Curriculum success condition

The pedagogical project is successful when a ten-year-old comes away with a clear, usable mental picture of:

- completed intelligibility;
- the 6 Transformation Patterns;
- the 3 Completion Topologies;
- the 3 Persistence Modes;
- the distinction among the three dimensions;
- Dimensional Conjunction;
- the 54-position grammar;
- neighbouring-cell discrimination;
- the possibility of “no coordinate” where no completion has occurred;
- the difference between intelligibility and truth;
- the ability to recognize and discuss these structures in ordinary life.

Everything else in this repository is a route toward that outcome.

**Do not simplify away the structure. Simplify the route into it.**
